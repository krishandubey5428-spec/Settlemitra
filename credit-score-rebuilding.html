Understanding Credit Scores in India

A credit score is a three-digit number that represents your creditworthiness — essentially, how likely you are to repay borrowed money. In India, credit scores are maintained by four major credit bureaus: CIBIL (TransUnion CIBIL), Experian, Equifax, and CRIF High Mark. Among these, the CIBIL score is the most widely referenced by lenders when evaluating loan and credit card applications. Credit scores in India range from 300 to 900, with higher scores indicating better creditworthiness.

A score above 750 is generally considered good and gives you access to the best interest rates and highest approval chances for loans and credit cards. Scores between 650 and 750 are considered fair — you can still get credit, but you may face higher interest rates or stricter terms. Scores below 650 are considered poor and make it very difficult to obtain credit from mainstream lenders. A score below 500 typically means you will be rejected by most banks and NBFCs.

Your credit score is calculated based on several factors. Payment history carries the most weight — approximately 35% of your score — and reflects whether you have made loan and credit card payments on time. Credit utilization, which measures how much of your available credit you are using, accounts for about 25%. The length of your credit history contributes roughly 15%, while the mix of credit types (secured versus unsecured, revolving versus installment) and the number of recent credit inquiries each contribute about 10% to 15%.

Understanding these factors is crucial for rebuilding your score because it tells you exactly where to focus your efforts. After a settlement, your payment history is the most affected component. The settlement is recorded on your credit report and signals to future lenders that you did not repay the full amount owed. However, by building a new track record of consistent, on-time payments and managing credit responsibly, you can gradually offset this negative mark and improve your score over time.

How Settlement Affects Your Credit Score

When a loan or credit card debt is settled — meaning you paid less than the full outstanding amount to close the account — the lender reports the account status to credit bureaus as "settled" rather than "closed." This distinction is important because a "settled" status indicates that the lender took a loss on the account and that you did not fulfill your original repayment obligation in full. This is viewed negatively by credit scoring models and by future lenders evaluating your applications.

The immediate impact of a settlement on your credit score depends on your score before the settlement process began. If your score was already low due to missed payments and defaults — which is the case for most borrowers who go through settlement — the additional impact of the settlement itself may be relatively modest, perhaps 30 to 75 points. However, if by some unusual circumstance your score was relatively intact before settlement, the drop could be more significant.

It is important to understand that the "settled" status remains on your credit report for seven years from the date of settlement. During this time, any lender who pulls your credit report will see this entry. However, the impact of this entry diminishes over time. Credit scoring models give more weight to recent behavior than to older entries. This means that as you build a new, positive credit history after settlement, the negative effect of the settled account gradually reduces, even though the entry itself remains visible.

There are also steps you can take to address the settled status directly. In some cases, after completing a settlement and demonstrating improved financial behavior, you can approach the lender and request that they update the account status from "settled" to "closed" in exchange for paying the remaining difference. While lenders are not obligated to do this, some will agree, particularly if you make a reasonable case. Timeline for Credit Score Recovery

One of the most common questions we hear is, "How long will it take to rebuild my credit score?" The honest answer is that it depends on your starting point, the severity of the negative entries on your report, and how consistently you follow the rebuilding strategy. However, based on our experience working with hundreds of clients, here is a realistic timeline for what you can expect:

Months 1 to 3: Foundation building. During the first three months, the focus is on analyzing your credit report, disputing errors, and setting up the foundational credit building products such as a secured credit card or credit builder loan. You may see a modest score improvement of 20 to 40 points if errors are corrected, but the primary goal in this phase is establishing the habits and products that will drive long-term improvement.

Months 4 to 6: Early progress. By this point, your secured credit card or credit builder loan has been reporting to credit bureaus for a few months. If you have been making all payments on time and keeping credit utilization below 30%, you should start seeing consistent upward movement in your score. An improvement of 40 to 80 points from your post-settlement baseline is achievable during this phase.

Months 7 to 12: Significant improvement. With six to twelve months of positive payment history established, the improvement becomes more pronounced. Your score may increase by 80 to 150 points from its lowest point, depending on your starting level and how disciplined you have been. At this stage, some clients become eligible for unsecured credit cards with modest limits and personal loans at higher-than-average interest rates.

Months 13 to 24: Strong recovery. After a full year or more of consistent positive credit behavior, many of our clients achieve scores in the 650 to 750 range, which opens the door to mainstream credit products with competitive interest rates. The settlement entry on your credit report continues to age and carries less weight in scoring models as your recent positive history grows. By the end of 24 months, most clients have rebuilt their credit to a functional level that supports their financial goals.

Strategies for Improving Your Credit Score

Rebuilding your credit score requires a multi-pronged approach. Here are the key strategies that Settlemitra recommends and implements as part of our rebuilding program:

Pay every bill on time, every time. Payment history is the single most important factor in your credit score, accounting for approximately 35% of the total. After a settlement, establishing a perfect payment record is absolutely essential. Set up automatic payments or reminders for every financial obligation — credit cards, utility bills, phone bills, EMIs, and any other recurring payments. Even one missed payment during the rebuilding phase can set your progress back significantly.

Keep credit utilization below 30%. Credit utilization — the percentage of your available credit that you are actually using — is the second most important scoring factor. If you have a secured credit card with a ₹25,000 limit, try to keep your outstanding balance below ₹7,500 at all times. Lower utilization signals to credit scoring models that you are managing credit responsibly and are not overextended. Ideally, aim for utilization between 10% and 20% for the best score impact.

Maintain existing credit accounts. If you have any credit accounts that are in good standing — such as an old credit card that you have been paying on time — do not close them. The length of your credit history matters, and closing old accounts shortens your average account age, which can lower your score. Keep these accounts active with small, regular purchases that you pay off in full each month.

Limit new credit applications. Every time you apply for credit, the lender performs a "hard inquiry" on your credit report, which temporarily reduces your score by a few points. During the rebuilding phase, be strategic about credit applications. Only apply for products you are likely to be approved for, and space applications at least three to six months apart. Multiple applications in a short period signal desperation to lenders and scoring models.

Diversify your credit mix gradually. Having a healthy mix of credit types — such as a credit card (revolving credit) and a small loan (installment credit) — can positively impact your score. However, do not take on new debt just for the sake of diversification. As your score improves, opportunities for different credit products will naturally become available. Settlemitra will advise you on the right time to add new credit types to your profile.

Secured Credit Cards and Credit Builder Loans

Two of the most effective tools for rebuilding credit after settlement are secured credit cards and credit builder loans. These products are specifically designed for individuals with low credit scores or limited credit history, making them ideal for post-settlement rebuilding.

Secured credit cards work by requiring you to deposit a fixed amount with the issuing bank, which serves as collateral. Your credit limit is typically equal to or a percentage of your deposit. For example, if you deposit ₹25,000, you may receive a credit card with a ₹25,000 limit. Because the bank's risk is covered by your deposit, approval is much easier regardless of your current credit score. The key benefit is that the card is reported to credit bureaus just like any regular credit card. By using it responsibly — making purchases and paying the full balance on time each month — you build a positive payment history that directly improves your credit score.

Several major banks in India offer secured credit cards, including SBI, ICICI Bank, Axis Bank, and Kotak Mahindra Bank. Settlemitra helps you compare options, choose the most suitable card based on your financial situation, and provides guidance on optimal usage patterns for maximum score improvement. We recommend using the card for small, regular purchases — such as groceries or utility bill payments — and paying the full balance before the due date every single month.

Credit builder loans are small, fixed-term loans specifically designed to help borrowers establish or rebuild credit. The loan amount is typically held in a fixed deposit or escrow account while you make monthly EMI payments. Once you have made all payments, the funds are released to you. The primary purpose is not to provide you with money — it is to create a track record of on-time loan payments that is reported to credit bureaus. This adds installment credit to your profile and demonstrates reliable repayment behavior.

Credit builder loans are offered by several NBFCs and fintech lenders in India. The amounts are usually small — ₹10,000 to ₹50,000 — with tenures of 6 to 12 months. Settlemitra identifies the best credit builder loan options available to you based on your current credit profile and financial capacity, and guides you through the application and repayment process to ensure maximum positive impact on your score.

Monitoring Your Credit Report

Regular monitoring of your credit report is a critical component of the rebuilding process. Knowing what is on your report, catching errors early, and tracking your progress keeps you informed and motivated. Here is how to effectively monitor your credit during the rebuilding phase:

Obtain free annual credit reports. Under RBI regulations, each of the four major credit bureaus — CIBIL, Experian, Equifax, and CRIF High Mark — is required to provide you with one free credit report per year. This means you can obtain four free reports annually, effectively allowing you to check your credit every quarter at no cost. Take advantage of this by staggering your requests — pull one report every three months from a different bureau.

Review every entry carefully. When you receive your credit report, do not just look at the score — examine every line item. Check that all account statuses are correctly reported, that there are no duplicate entries, that settled accounts are accurately reflected, and that there are no accounts you do not recognize (which could indicate identity theft or errors). Any discrepancy, no matter how small, should be disputed immediately.

Track score changes over time. Keep a record of your credit score each time you check it. This allows you to see trends and understand how your actions are affecting your score. A steadily rising score confirms that your rebuilding strategy is working. If you notice unexpected drops, investigate the cause immediately — it could be an error on your report, a missed payment you were not aware of, or a new negative entry that needs to be addressed.

Use credit monitoring services. Several platforms in India offer free or low-cost credit monitoring services that alert you to changes in your credit report. Services like CIBIL's credit monitoring subscription, Experian's credit alerts, and various fintech platforms provide real-time notifications when new accounts are opened, inquiries are made, or significant score changes occur. Settlemitra recommends using at least one monitoring service during the rebuilding phase for proactive awareness.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Rebuilding credit after settlement requires patience and discipline. Many borrowers unintentionally slow their recovery by making avoidable mistakes. Here are the most common pitfalls to watch out for:

Applying for too much credit at once. After going through the settlement process, many borrowers are eager to re-establish their credit and apply for multiple loans or credit cards simultaneously. Each application triggers a hard inquiry on your credit report, and multiple inquiries in a short period signal financial desperation to scoring models and lenders. This can actually lower your score and reduce your chances of approval. Instead, be strategic — apply for one product at a time, starting with secured options, and wait at least three to six months between applications.

Ignoring your credit report. Some borrowers settle their debt and then avoid looking at their credit report, either out of fear or disinterest. This is a mistake because errors on your report can go undetected and continue dragging your score down. Regular monitoring allows you to catch and correct these errors promptly. It also helps you track your progress and stay motivated during the long rebuilding process.

Maxing out new credit products. If you obtain a secured credit card, do not use the full available limit. High credit utilization — even on a secured card — negatively impacts your score. Keep your spending to no more than 30% of the credit limit, and ideally below 20%. Using the full limit suggests financial strain and works against the positive signal you are trying to build.

Missing payments on any obligation. During the rebuilding phase, even a single missed payment on any financial obligation — whether it is a credit card, utility bill, or loan EMI — can have a disproportionately negative impact on your score. Set up automatic payments for everything and maintain an emergency buffer in your bank account to ensure that payments are never missed due to insufficient funds.

Falling for credit repair scams. Be wary of companies or individuals who promise to "fix" your credit score quickly or guarantee specific score improvements. Legitimate credit rebuilding takes time and consistent effort. No one can legally remove accurate negative information from your credit report, and anyone who promises otherwise is likely running a scam. Work only with reputable firms like Settlemitra that provide transparent, realistic guidance.

Closing old accounts prematurely. If you have any credit accounts with a long history that are in good standing, do not close them. The age of your credit accounts contributes to your overall score. Closing an old account reduces your average account age and decreases your total available credit, both of which can negatively impact your score. Keep old accounts open and use them occasionally to keep them active.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a debt settlement lower my credit score?

The impact varies based on your score before settlement. If your score was already low due to missed payments and defaults (which is common for borrowers going through settlement), the additional drop from the settlement itself may be 30 to 75 points. The account will be marked as "settled" on your credit report, which is viewed negatively by lenders. However, since most borrowers in settlement already have damaged scores, the settlement itself is often a relatively small additional impact compared to the missed payments that preceded it.

How long does the "settled" status stay on my credit report?

The settled status remains on your credit report for seven years from the date of settlement. However, its impact on your score diminishes over time as you build new, positive credit history. Credit scoring models give significantly more weight to recent behavior than to older entries. After two to three years of consistent positive credit behavior, the practical impact of the settled entry on your score and your ability to obtain credit is substantially reduced.

Can I get the "settled" status changed to "closed" on my report?

In some cases, yes. After completing a settlement, you can approach the lender and offer to pay the remaining difference between the settlement amount and the original outstanding balance in exchange for updating the account status from "settled" to "closed." Lenders are not obligated to agree, but some will, particularly if you present a reasonable request and the amount involved is manageable. Settlemitra can advise you on whether this approach is worthwhile in your situation and assist with the negotiation.

What credit score do I need to get a loan after settlement?

Most mainstream banks and NBFCs require a minimum CIBIL score of 650 to 700 for personal loans and 700 to 750 for home loans. For credit cards, the threshold is typically lower, around 600 to 650 for basic cards. Secured credit cards and credit builder loans are available to borrowers with scores below 600, which is why they are the starting point of our rebuilding program. How long does it take to rebuild a credit score after settlement?

With disciplined effort and proper strategy, most borrowers see meaningful improvement within 6 to 12 months and achieve a functional score (650 or above) within 12 to 24 months. The exact timeline depends on your starting score, the number and severity of negative entries on your report, and how consistently you follow the rebuilding plan.
